01

Deferred light and shadows

A three-target material G-buffer feeds GGX direct lighting. Stochastic points on the sun disk and local-light sphere launch low-count rays through the directional atlas and radial cube depth; automatic receiver bias and world-locked sampling keep them stable. A PCF path remains available for direct comparison.

02

Faithful probe cache

The C++ baker captures static-scene irradiance into 720 L1 directional probes. Invalid samples are rejected and flood-filled, while six directional reach values suppress interpolation through walls. Moving objects receive—but never rewrite—the bake.

03

Screen trace and denoise

Half-resolution cosine rays march the camera depth representation. Misses fall back to volume probes. Spatial reuse, world-position temporal reprojection, neighbourhood clamping, and bilateral weights stabilize one or two rays per pixel.

04

Stable specular probes

Glossy indirect lighting uses only a mip-filtered sphere probe; screen-space reflections are deliberately disabled. The probe can be recaptured explicitly and remains separate from diffuse volume probes, preserving EEVEE’s split indirect-light architecture.

Scope and fidelity

This is an architectural WebGL2 reconstruction, not Blender source compiled for the browser. WebGL2 cannot expose EEVEE’s compute-driven sparse virtual-page allocator, so the exhibit preserves its important behaviour with a fixed clip-atlas analogue. Its soft-shadow path follows EEVEE’s light-shape sampling and light-to-receiver depth traversal, but uses fixed-resident single-layer maps rather than Blender’s sparse VSM pages and compute passes. It does preserve the baked/dynamic boundary: volume probes are static, direct lights and shadows are live, screen traces see only camera-visible information, and probe fallback fills misses.
