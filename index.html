Slate Ocean — how it works

🌊 Spectral wave field (FFT)

The open-ocean surface is synthesized from a physical wave spectrum with an inverse FFT on the GPU (Tessendorf 1999 + JONSWAP/TMA oceanographic spectra). Wind speed, fetch and depth set the energy; swell trains are added as narrow directional lobes. Three cascades (512 / 128 / 32 m tiles) partition the spectrum so wavelengths from ~200 m down to ~25 cm are all resolved.

🚫 Wave cancellation

Water is linear for small waves: heights add. A canceller buoy senses the passing swell with an up-wave probe, fits amplitude + phase (adaptive least-squares, like active noise control), then emits the anti-phase wave — leaving a calm shadow down-wave. Place probes A (up-wave) and B (down-wave) to measure the attenuation in dB. Real systems work exactly this way (dipole absorbers, 2024).

🏄 Surf & breaking waves

Near shore the simulator applies finite-depth physics: group-velocity shoaling (Green's law), depth-limited crest height (Miche ≈ 0.78·D), crest steepening and a pitching lip. Pick Pipeline (reef barrel), Mavericks (deep big-wave reef) or Beach break and watch sets peak, feather and detonate over the bathymetry.

🫧 Foam & spray particles

Whitecaps are not a static texture: a persistent foam field is advected by the measured surface velocity, fed where the surface folds (Jacobian), breaks, or runs up the beach, and decays over seconds. Up to 262k GPU spray/foam particles are ballistically integrated with drag, spawned at folding crests and pitching lips.

📏 Probes

Click the water to drop probe A, then B. Probes read back true GPU wave height and report RMS height, significant height Hs and peak period from zero-crossings. Buoys ride the same field.
