# Field references

The model uses Golden Eagle references rather than a generic bird approximation. These sources were checked while building the silhouette and animation timing.

1. **Cornell Lab of Ornithology — Golden Eagle lifting off and flying through snow**  
   https://academy.allaboutbirds.org/golden-eagle-lifting-off-and-flying-through-the-snow/  
   The transcript documents the observable sequence used for the powered-flight pose: a leap from a perch, several beats while the legs tuck underneath, then a repeating flap–flap–glide sequence. It also notes the fully fanned tail used for steering and the head shake / feather settling visible before takeoff.

2. **Birds of the World — Golden Eagle, *Aquila chrysaetos*, behavior**  
   https://birdsoftheworld.org/bow/species/goleag/cur/behavior  
   The locomotion notes describe the grounded walk as awkward, with great strides, a rolling body motion, shuffling wings for balance, and a low, nearly horizontal head/body carriage. The flight notes describe deep flapping sessions interspersed with shorter glides.

3. **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Pepper W. Trail — Identification of Bald and Golden Eagle Feathers**  
   https://www.originalwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/bsk-pdf-manager/2019/03/Trail_2014_Identification-of-bald-and-golden-eagle-feathers.pdf  
   The feather layout is based on this guide: the outer ten wing feathers are primaries, the inner flight feathers are secondaries, Golden Eagle primaries have pointed / notched outer tips, secondaries are rounded, and eagles have twelve tail feathers. The adult Golden Eagle's brown marbling and dark outer primaries informed the material variation.

4. **American Ornithological Society — Fly Like an Eagle?**  
   https://americanornithology.org/fly-like-an-eagle/  
   Used for the flight profile: Golden Eagles primarily soar with wings held outward and use rising air rather than continuous flapping.

5. **National Eagle Center — Eagle glossary**  
   https://www.nationaleaglecenter.org/eagle-glossary/  
   Used for the alula / flight vocabulary and the distinction between gliding and soaring.

6. **Cornell Lab of Ornithology — Golden Eagle species account**  
   https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle/id  
   Used as a general field-identification cross-check for the adult Golden Eagle silhouette and plumage.

## Scope note

The animation is a reference-led procedural study, not a motion-capture claim. It deliberately exposes the forms and timing in an inspectable browser scene; any production shot that requires frame-accurate biomechanics should be validated against high-speed footage of the selected individual, because wingbeat amplitude, cadence, and walking gait vary with speed, wind, sex, age, and terrain.
