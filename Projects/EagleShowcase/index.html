Reference

Skeleton — bald eagle long-bone means (Royal BC Museum avian osteology): humerus 207 mm, ulna 238 mm, radius 227 mm, carpometacarpus 112 mm, tarsometatarsus 96 mm. Wingspan 1.8–2.3 m, body 0.70–1.02 m, tail 0.23–0.37 m, mass 3.0–6.3 kg.

Plumage — Accipitridae counts: 10 primaries, 17 secondaries (4 tertials), 3 alula quills, 12 rectrices, plus primary / greater / median / lesser / marginal / underwing covert rows and scapulars. Every one of them is a separate 3-D solid with its own joint.

Wingbeat — XROMM skeletal kinematics of flapping flight (Baier, Gatesy & Dial, PLOS ONE 2013): elbow peak extension ≈ 45 % of downstroke and peak flexion ≈ 45 % of upstroke (range ≈ 91°); the wrist holds extension into early upstroke then flexes fast, adducting ≈ 70°; elbow and wrist reverse ≈ 4 % and 2 % of the cycle before the wingtip. Cruise beat here: 2.4 Hz, 54 % downstroke.

Secondary motion is simulated, not keyframed: every feather, neck joint and the tail base carries an angular spring driven by its own bone's measured tip velocity.
